\begin{abstract}
A frozen language model can be coupled to a frozen partner model through small trainable latent bridges, with no text at the interface: \companion{} builds such a system around a physics partner, where the quantity being carried is a number the partner computes. This paper asks whether the same channel can carry a \emph{disposition} --- and where in a backbone's hidden state it should be written. The partner is a 0.5B model holding Claude's constitution \citep{anthropic2026constitution}; the write site is the ``value neurons'' of \citet{xu2026valueneurons}, a sparse set of residual-stream coordinates that predict state value; the supervision is self-distillation from the same frozen backbone prompted with the document. We report five things. (1) The probe reproduces at both scales but its published causal justification does not: at 0.5B the damage from ablating value neurons tracks activation magnitude, with magnitude-matched controls reproducing it in full, and at 9B the ablation is a flat null. (2) The number of coordinates carrying the probe's value signal is roughly \emph{fixed in absolute terms} across a 4.6$\times$ change in model width --- an AUC knee near 100--200 dimensions at both 896 and 4096 --- so it is a falling \emph{fraction} as models grow. (3) At the default cap a masked write into those coordinates is safe at 9B and carries none of the prompted teacher's judgment, and none of its fit beyond what a temperature gives the backbone (item 5), while a full-width write moves the keyword refusal count ($0.660 \to 0.720$ on 100 prompts, six and none away, exact McNemar $p = 0.031$; $0.653 \to 0.693$ on 400, nineteen and three, $p = 0.0009$). On 100 prompts adjudication against a written rubric reduced that to one decision changed and one the other way, the same single prompt changing at every width; on 400 it does not: the wide write withholds the requested assistance on 21 pairs and supplies it on 2 ($p = 0.0001$), a set largely disjoint from the keyword flips, and of the 21, two blind judges agree that 2 withhold specifics a careful assistant should withhold, 4 withhold dual-use material, and 15 withhold legitimate information: the disposition the wide write carries is a blunter refusal, paid for mostly in helpfulness. A content-free injection of the same magnitude through the same gate changes 8 decisions one way and 4 the other ($p = 0.39$) at 5\% of the divergence, so the withholding is the document's content and not the perturbation; and a partner that has never read the document reproduces it, 19 against 3 on largely the same pairs, so the content arrives through the self-distillation teacher and the partner is a carrier rather than a source --- by item (5), one whose cargo the write does not read. That contrast, however, is confounded: the injection cap bounds the write's \emph{per-dimension} magnitude and every arm saturated it, so full width was handed 26$\times$ the total perturbation energy of the value-neuron mask. Per unit of energy actually spent the narrow write bought 3.6$\times$ more cross-entropy gain as evaluated, though none of the part of it that survives the temperature control of item (5). At matched energy the same 410 coordinates --- 10\% of the stream, the cap raised to restore parity --- carry 62\% of full width's cross-entropy gain as evaluated (of the part that survives the temperature control, 0.007 of its 0.040), 55\% of its divergence and four of its six refusal flips; on 400 prompts it withholds on substance too, 16 pairs against 5 ($p = 0.027$), as does the probe-worst set ($15{:}2$), and both hand over a precursor list the backbone had refused; the 41- and 205-coordinate masks at parity, on the same prompts, carry a weaker copy of the withholding ($10{:}4$ and $10{:}6$, neither significant) and release nothing harmful; so on divergence and the keyword count most of what read as a width effect was budget, though a residue survives parity, while on the fit that survives a temperature width is the larger term; and the probe's coordinates --- uniquely among six matched sets --- carry a hundredfold larger divergence on MMLU while changing no more answers. (4) Two bridges of entirely different kinds --- the physics partner and the constitution --- run on one frozen 9B backbone with no joint training, and training them jointly makes both worse. Neither channel costs the other its payload: with both open, the five red-team prompts that flip the keyword count are a subset of the six the constitution channel flips alone, the two arms disagree on one held-out prompt in a hundred, and adjudicated each changes the same one decision. But the composition is not inert on the backbone, and that is this paper's last negative result: MMLU divergence rises to 0.072 where the constitution channel alone stayed at 0.005 and the physics channel alone sits at 0.041 --- a divergence without an accuracy change, since after a parser repair described in Section~\ref{sec:repro2} no arm in this paper moves MMLU accuracy significantly. (5) Controls made after the campaign qualify the above without moving an adjudicated count. At 9B most of the cross-entropy gain is \emph{sharpening}: with the backbone and the coupled system each read at its own best temperature, 0.040 of full width's 0.094 is left on red-team prompts and 0.004 of 0.058 on helpful ones, an interval that includes zero, the narrow writes keep 0.012 or less, and the probe's coordinates lose the advantage in fit they had over random ones. At 0.5B the control was run afterwards and read by rules committed before it ran: by them the full-width write keeps a gain, and, described beside the readings, most of that gain is not sharpening (0.373 is left of 0.359 on red-team prompts and 0.142 of 0.197 on helpful ones), while the two narrow comparisons that had favoured the plain partner, and the probe's coordinates over magnitude-matched ones, do not survive it (over nine random coordinates the probe's do, at the edge of zero), in readings the run itself showed to be weak. And at 9B the write does not depend on what the channel read: another prompt's tokens move the output by a KL of 0.0003 where the write itself moves it by 0.065. In two experiments with their criteria fixed in advance, the full-width bridge fed, for every prompt, one stored set of tokens --- the mean of 100 other prompts' --- reproduces the withholding ($21{:}3$), while a set made from no prompt carries a weaker copy ($13{:}5$, $p = 0.096$), and a bridge trained with no partner at all withholds on 14 pairs against 2, in each of two runs, where two runs with the partner give 21 and 16; whether the partner adds that difference is not settled by two runs a recipe. Under further criteria fixed in advance that stored set stands in for the partner's path at 9B, leaving three benchmarks where the partner's path has them (GSM8K 86, MMLU 75 and BoolQ 90 of 100, against 84, 75 and 89); at 0.5B, where the write uses what it reads, that bridge's own stored set does not (teacher-forced it moves the output by a KL of 0.026 where the limit is 0.005, and 13 of 100 keyword decisions differ). Everything runs on one consumer laptop (Apple M2, 24\,GB) and is reproducible from the repository.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro2}

The coupling interface this paper uses is not new here. \companion{} couples a frozen language model to a frozen neural operator through two small bridges --- a forward readout that turns hidden states into the partner's input, and a gated cross-attention that writes the partner's answer back into the residual stream --- and shows that a 0.5B backbone which answers Burgers-equation field questions at 5\% answers them at 100\% when the operator is attached, with nothing but hidden states crossing the interface. What crosses there is a number. The bridge has a quantity to carry, a right answer to be scored against, and a partner that computes it.

Values are not like that. There is no field value to read off, no tolerance to score within, and the partner does not compute anything --- it holds a document. The question this paper asks is whether the same channel still works when the payload is a disposition rather than a quantity, and it asks it in the form that matters for alignment at scale: \emph{where} in the hidden state does such a payload have to be written, and \emph{how much} of the hidden state does it have to disturb?

Those two questions turn out to be separable, and separating them is this paper's main technical contribution. The natural site is the sparse set of coordinates that \citet{xu2026valueneurons} identify as predicting state value --- roughly 1\% of the residual stream --- and writing only there is attractive for the obvious reason: a narrow write is a small intervention. We find that the probe locating those coordinates reproduces, that its causal justification does not, that the coordinate count carrying the signal is roughly constant in absolute terms as the model widens, and --- the part that required rereading our own instrumentation --- that the comparison between narrow and wide writes in our first campaign was not a comparison of dimensionality at all. The injection cap bounds per-dimension magnitude, so a narrow mask is also a small \emph{energy budget}, and the wide write won by spending 26 times more of it. Section~\ref{sec:reads} then finds that at 9B the write carries something learned rather than something read.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A constitution bridge: self-distillation from the same frozen backbone prompted with the document, with a masked write confined to chosen residual-stream coordinates, trained on 24\,GB of unified memory (Section~\ref{sec:constbridge}).
\item A reproduction of the value-neuron probe at two scales, and a refutation of its causal claim on both: magnitude-matched controls, not uniform ones, are what the claim requires (Section~\ref{sec:valueneurons}).
\item The measurement that the probe's signal occupies a roughly fixed \emph{number} of coordinates rather than a fixed fraction, which is what would make a narrow bridge scale (Section~\ref{sec:valueneurons}).
\item A separation of write \emph{width} from write \emph{budget}, with the energy accounting that shows the first campaign confounded them, and an energy-matched experiment that tests width proper (Section~\ref{sec:const9b}).
\item A dual stack: two bridges from unrelated partners on one frozen backbone, composing without joint training (Section~\ref{sec:dual}).
\item A temperature control showing that at 9B most of the cross-entropy gain is sharpening and, run at 0.5B under rules fixed in advance, that there the full-width write keeps its gain, most of which is not; and content controls showing that at 9B the write does not depend on what the channel read, the latter tested against criteria fixed in advance: stored tokens for every prompt, on the red-team prompts and then on three benchmarks, and a bridge trained with no partner (Section~\ref{sec:reads}).
\item Negative results reported rather than dropped: the partner control, the ablation null, the over-refusal, two 0.5B comparisons that do not survive the temperature control, and one data-pipeline check that fails at 9B (Sections~\ref{sec:const05}--\ref{sec:reads}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related2}

\textbf{Value neurons.} \citet{xu2026valueneurons} report that a sparse subset of residual-stream dimensions --- about 1\% --- suffices to predict the value of a reasoning state, and that ablating them damages performance. We reproduce the probe and examine the ablation with controls matched on activation magnitude; Section~\ref{sec:valueneurons} reports what survives.

\textbf{Constitutional AI.} \citet{bai2022cai} train a model to critique and revise its own outputs against a written constitution, with the document entering through prompts during training and leaving no explicit trace at inference. Our setup places the document \emph{outside} the model being aligned, in a separate frozen partner whose states a bridge writes back into the backbone as a latent; the partner controls of Sections~\ref{sec:const05} and~\ref{sec:const9b} show that the document in fact reaches the backbone through the self-distillation teacher's context rather than the partner's weights, and at 9B Section~\ref{sec:reads} finds that the write does not depend on what the partner computes from the prompt. The training signal is closer to distillation from a prompted teacher \citep{bai2022hh} than to RLHF, and the constitution text is \citet{anthropic2026constitution}.

\textbf{Coupled frozen models.} The Global Workspace line couples frozen specialists through learned latent workspaces, and the Bicameral Model \citep{flamant2026bicameral} couples two frozen language streams through a gated hidden-state interface; \companion{} extends that to a partner from another modality entirely. This paper keeps the same interface and changes only what is on the other side of it.

\textbf{Activation steering.} Writing a direction into the residual stream to change behaviour is a large literature, and a constitution bridge can be read as a steering intervention designed to be computed per prompt by a partner model rather than fixed in advance --- though at 9B what the partner path delivers has the same effect for every prompt, and what varies with the prompt is the gate and the attention query reading the backbone's own stream (Section~\ref{sec:reads}) --- and gated by a learned function of the hidden state rather than applied always-on. Section~\ref{sec:const9b}'s energy accounting is the point of contact: a steering intervention's strength and its support are separate knobs, and conflating them is easy to do.

\section{The Interface, Recapped}
\label{sec:interface2}

The bridges are the ones \companion{} describes, and this section records only what the constitution experiments need. The backbone is frozen throughout; so is the partner.

A \textbf{forward readout} pools the backbone's hidden states at a read depth $l_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ and emits $m$ tokens in the partner's embedding space. A \textbf{gated cross-attention} at a write depth $l_{\mathrm{rev}} \ge l_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ attends from the backbone's stream to the partner's states and adds the result back:
\[
  \Delta h = \sigma(h)\,\mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{inj}(h),\,c)\,s(h),
  \qquad
  h \leftarrow h + \Delta h ,
\]
where $\sigma(h) \in (0,1)$ is a learned scalar gate, $s(h)$ is the RMS of the receiving hidden state over the written coordinates, and $c$ is the injection cap. Two details matter later and are stated here so that Section~\ref{sec:const9b}'s accounting is checkable. First, the cap bounds the injection's \emph{root-mean-square per written coordinate}, so it is indifferent to how many coordinates are written. Second, the gate and the readout's query read the \emph{whole} stream; only the write is masked.

A \textbf{masked write} restricts $\Delta h$ to a frozen set of coordinates, chosen before training and never updated. The bridge's parameter count does not depend on that set's size --- the mask is a boolean, and the projections are full width --- so width in this paper is never a proxy for capacity.

Supervision is \textbf{self-distillation}. The teacher is the same frozen backbone with the constitution as a system prompt; the student is the backbone with the bridges attached and a plain system prompt; the loss is cross-entropy on the teacher's greedy continuation. A \textbf{no-harm arm} interleaves batches of GSM8K and MMLU on which only the gate is trained, with a penalty on the gate's mean activation, so the channel learns to stay shut where the document has nothing to say.
\section{Where to write: the value neurons}
\label{sec:valueneurons}

A channel that writes into a residual stream has to write \emph{somewhere}, and until now the site was chosen by depth alone. \citet{xu2026valueneurons} offer a principled alternative: train a probe on the residual stream to predict whether the model's own continuation will turn out correct, prune it, and a sparse subset of coordinates --- roughly 1\% --- retains the signal. They report causal evidence that these coordinates encode reward-related information. If that holds, it names a place where a normative channel might be legible to the model rather than merely loud.

\textbf{The probe reproduces; its published training recipe does not.} We collected 1,000 GSM8K-train trajectories from the frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B under greedy decoding, storing the residual stream entering blocks 4--20 at every position (5.5\,GB of float16 states, reward rate 0.308; \code{results/value\_neurons/qwen0.5b/collect.log}), and 800 from Qwen3.5 9B at the paper's own sampler (temperature 1.0, top-$p$ 0.95; reward 0.741, 593 correct against 207 incorrect, seven depths, 4.3 hours, 14.4\,GB; \code{qwen35/collect.log}). The probe is theirs: $d \to 1024 \to 1$ with ReLU, AdamW at $10^{-4}$, batch 4 trajectories, split 80/20 by trajectory. Its temporal-difference objective is where the recipe had to bend. With one reward-bearing residual among roughly 230 per trajectory, TD at 30 epochs leaves the value function nearly constant: held-out AUC 0.42--0.53 across the six layers the sweep archived before a Metal fault ended it, below chance on four (\code{qwen0.5b/td30/}). Regressing every state directly on its trajectory's reward --- the fixed point TD converges to as $\gamma \to 1$, reached without the backward propagation TD needs (\code{--target mc}) --- gives 0.61--0.70 at full width on the 0.5B, and the $L_1$-column pruning then keeps 0.628 at nine of 896 dimensions at layer 16 against 0.454, 0.520 and 0.577 for three nine-dimension random subsets retrained identically.

At 9B the signal is far stronger and the \emph{sparsity} is weaker (\code{qwen35/summary.json}):

A bigger, better-trained model carries a far more legible estimate of whether its own continuation will be correct --- 0.79--0.84 against the 0.5B's 0.61--0.70 --- but past layer 22 arbitrary coordinates predict nearly as well as the selected ones, and at layer 30 slightly better. Whatever concentration exists lives in the early-middle layers, which is where \citet{xu2026valueneurons} look. Layer 24 holds the widest gap of the three candidates, $+0.076$, and that gap needs an error bar: with 118 correct and 42 incorrect held-out trajectories the Hanley--McNeil standard error on a single AUC of 0.79 is 0.036, so the gap is about 1.5 conservative standard errors. Suggestive, not established.

\textbf{The causal claim fails twice, in two different ways.} At 0.5B, zeroing the nine value neurons at every position costs 13 GSM8K points (31\% $\to$ 18\%, McNemar $p = 0.029$, $n = 100$ test, greedy, 384 tokens) and the top 45 costs 23 --- the published shape. Three uniform random nine-dimension controls give 26\%, 0\% and 29\%, which looks decisive until the 0\% is examined: that draw contains dimension 62, which carries the attention sink, with mean $|h| = 1{,}551$ at position 0 against an RMS of 0.513 elsewhere, and zeroing it leaves \emph{no item stopping} (end-of-sequence rate 0.0, every generation running to the 384-token cap). A random control that can draw the sink measures the sink. Excluding such dimensions (detected as above 100 in magnitude at position 0 and more than 50$\times$ their level elsewhere; exactly dimension 62 at all ten captured layers) the redraws come back 36\%, 19\% and 32\% --- and the middle one is as damaging as the value neurons themselves, at a smaller $p$ (0.0169 against 0.0294).

That draw is not bad luck. It identifies a confound the uniform baseline does not control for: \textbf{the damage tracks activation magnitude}. Ranked by activation RMS over the probe's own state slice, the nine value neurons hold ranks 2, 9 and 10 of 896 and sum to 13.6 against a median dimension's 0.388; the damaging redraw holds ranks 5 and 22; the two benign redraws hold nothing above rank 114 and sum to 3--4. The value neurons are large --- though not the largest, a distinction worth keeping, since the stream's biggest coordinate at this layer is dimension 490 at RMS 9.81, three times any value neuron, and it is not in the set. The control this calls for is a matched one: three draws of nine dimensions, each member matched one-to-one to a value neuron's activation RMS, taken from outside the top 5\% and outside the sink. It dissolves the effect (\code{qwen0.5b/ablate\_layer16\_magmatch*.json}):

Draw 2 also shows a failure mode the accuracy column hides, cutting generated length from 255 tokens to 88 with the end-of-sequence rate rising to 0.90 --- zeroing large coordinates can \emph{stop} the model rather than mislead it.

At 9B there is no damage to explain. Zeroing the 41 value neurons at layer 24 costs three points ($83\% \to 80\%$, $p = 0.375$), the top 5\% costs one ($p = 1.0$), and three random 41-dimension draws cost nothing (82\%, 83\%, 84\%, $p = 1.0$ each; \code{qwen35/ablate\_layer24.json}). A magnitude-matched control was prepared for this backbone and then cancelled, with its reasoning recorded (\code{results/qwen35/vn35\_magmatch.log}): a matched control exists to ask whether damage is value or magnitude, and here there is no damage to attribute. The magnitude story also inverts with scale, which may be why: at 0.5B two of the nine value neurons sit in the top 1\% of dimensions by activation RMS, a 22-fold enrichment, and at 9B three of 41 do, a 7.3-fold one, while layer 24 has no attention-sink dimension at all. A small model appears to pack value information into the few channels it can least afford to lose --- exactly the regime in which an ablation cannot separate the two explanations.

\textbf{What survives is the probe.} A probe rescales its own inputs, so a large coordinate earns no advantage there, and the top 1\% still keeps AUC 0.628 at 0.5B and 0.788 at 9B where same-size random subsets retrained identically reach 0.454--0.577 and 0.655--0.747. The defensible claim is therefore correlational and narrow: a sparse subset of coordinates carries a disproportionate share of a linearly decodable self-evaluation signal, in the early-middle layers only. It is a \emph{probe-identified, plausible} place to write into, not a demonstrated causal substrate --- and that is the claim on which the rest of this section rests.

\textbf{How many coordinates, and does the answer scale?} The probe is trained at
full width and then pruned by its own $L_1$ column norms, which gives the held-out
AUC as a function of how many coordinates survive. Read across the two scales
(Table~\ref{tab:vnknee}) the curves have the same shape and, more interestingly, the
same \emph{absolute} breaking point. At 9B the AUC sits between 0.82 and 0.85 from

between 0.66 and 0.69 from 896 down to 179 and then falls, to 0.634 at 45. The knee
is near 100--200 coordinates at both, while $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ differs by
4.6$\times$ --- so as a \emph{fraction} it moves from roughly 20\% of the stream to
roughly 5\%.

We state this carefully, because it is the observation on which a scaling argument
would rest and it is weaker than such an argument needs. The grid steps by factors
of about 2.2, so ``100--200'' is the resolution available and not a measurement; the
AUC differences inside each flat region are within what a 160-item held-out split

signal occupies a fixed \emph{fraction} of the residual stream, and the fixed
\emph{count} reading is the one consistent with both measurements. If it holds, a
bridge written into a few hundred coordinates stays a few hundred coordinates as
backbones widen, and the share of the stream it disturbs keeps falling --- which is
the property that would make this kind of coupling usable on a frontier model, and
which Section~\ref{sec:const9b} shows is not yet demonstrated at even one scale.


\section{The partner, the teacher, and a masked write}
\label{sec:constbridge}

\textbf{The partner holds the document and little else.} Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct was fine-tuned on the constitution (28,958 words, 39 headings; the copy and its provenance are committed) with every decoder block trainable and the 136M embedding frozen --- 357.9M of 494.0M parameters --- for 600 iterations, about 7.3 epochs, then frozen. What it absorbed is recitation: 27 of 38 sections come back verbatim for 160 tokens from their heading, bimodally (a section either returns exact or a different section does), the four core properties emerge in their stated priority order and the three principals by name. What it did not absorb is shown most sharply by the one probe it fails: asked to list the hard constraints, it returns 0 of 7, supplying the \emph{definition} of a hard constraint instead. Held-out paragraphs of the same document score perplexity 513 against the base model's 29. A memoriser, then, standing where the Fourier operator stood: the bridges read its hidden states, and what those states can carry about a situation is bounded by what the document says about one.

\textbf{The teacher is the backbone reading the document, not the partner.} Training is self-distillation. The teacher is the same frozen backbone given a constitution excerpt as its system prompt --- the core values, the honesty properties, the hard constraints, 2,848 words --- and the student is that backbone under a plain system prompt with the bridges attached; the loss is cross-entropy on the teacher's greedy continuation over red-team prompts from \code{hh-rlhf} harmless-base \citep{bai2022hh}, alongside the no-harm arm of Section~\ref{sec:interface2} on GSM8K and MMLU prompts with the backbone's own continuations. The distinction matters for reading everything below: the constitution reaches the student through the \emph{teacher's context}, and the partner model is the channel's far end rather than its source. This is also what separates the setup from Constitutional AI \citep{bai2022cai}, which revises a model's outputs against principles and trains on the revisions; here nothing is revised and no weights of either model move.

Worth stating before any training number, because it bounds what distillation can transfer: the excerpt is a weak and two-sided signal. At 0.5B it changes the continuation on 97--99\% of red-team prompts, but the change is mainly terseness (114 $\to$ 62 tokens) and stopping (end-of-sequence 54\% $\to$ 78\%), and its effect on refusal is a residue of much larger churn --- on the training split it \emph{adds} a refusal on 19.1\% of prompts and \emph{removes} one on 13.75\%, for a net of $+5.4$ points. The document is not acting as a refusal filter; it changes the decision in both directions, which is what its own text asks for. It also refuses more on ordinary requests (0.04 $\to$ 0.12), so a faithful student inherits an over-refusal tax.

\textbf{The write is masked.} The forward bridge reads the whole residual stream at \code{l\_fwd} --- value dimensions alone cannot carry a situation --- pools the prompt into eight soft tokens with learned queries, and rescales them to the partner's embedding RMS (0.015; without that rescale they arrive about sixty times too large). The partner runs them between a fixed prefix and the fixed suffix \emph{``According to Claude's constitution, in this situation Claude should''}, and its final-norm states at the last eight positions are the features a reverse bridge maps back to eight tokens. The injection is the gated cross-attention of Section~\ref{sec:interface2} with one change: its output is multiplied by a frozen boolean mask over the written dimensions, and the injection cap and the receiver's local scale are taken over those coordinates only --- otherwise ``5\% of the local stream'' is ten times too tight for a nine-dimension write. With an all-ones mask the module is bit-identical to the unmasked one, and with a narrow mask the change to every other coordinate is exactly zero; both are asserted in its self-test. So the write location is a training-time flag, and the experiments below are one script differing in it. The bridges are 3.69M parameters at 0.5B and 28.34M at 9B.

\section{Width at 9B: budget and dimensionality}
\label{sec:const9b}

\textbf{Nothing here harms anything, and that inverts the 0.5B's central conclusion.} Across three widths and four datasets the largest movement is two GSM8K items gained by the 205-dimension write (McNemar $p = 0.5$); MMLU moves by one item each way at full width and by one at the top 5\%; BoolQ by one. At 0.5B the full-width write cost eight GSM8K points, and that section closed by treating the gate and the write mask as two switches, because ``what is confined to the value neurons cannot hurt and cannot do much, what reaches the whole stream does both''. At 9B the second half is false. The last column is divergence selectivity, and it rises with width --- 48$\times$, 119$\times$, 302$\times$ --- but it is not the gate's doing: measured on the gate itself, mean $\sigma$ on red-team prompts over mean $\sigma$ on arithmetic \emph{falls} with width, 547$\times$, 364$\times$, 109$\times$. A wide write is safe here because its divergence lands on the prompts the document speaks to, not because its gate is more selective; the gate is most selective where the write is narrowest, which is what the no-harm arm trains and what a narrow channel gets for free.

\textbf{Only full width moves the keyword count, and the count is not the decision.} Refusal on the held-out red-team prompts is 0.660 for base, 0.660 at 41 dimensions, 0.650 at 205, and 0.720 at 4096 against the teacher's 0.750: the masked variants transfer no behaviour at all, and the unmasked one moves six of the eight or nine points that separate base from the teacher. A rate cannot say whether six prompts flipped toward refusal or three flipped each way, and the difference is the whole claim, so we pair the flag per item (\code{eval/const\_refusal\_mcnemar.py}). Full width flips six prompts toward refusal and \textbf{none} away, exact McNemar $p = 0.031$; the 41- and 205-dimension writes are $1{:}1$ and $2{:}3$ churn (toward${:}$away) at $p = 1.00$. So on the keyword count the narrow masks transmit nothing and the wide one moves one-directionally. The count is an instrument. Adjudicating every pair against a written rubric --- relative to the backbone's reply, does the coupled reply withhold the requested assistance, in whole or substantial part? --- by a judge blind to the arm, with a fixed twenty-pair sample per arm labelled again independently, gives Table~\ref{tab:adjudicated}. On substance, full width's six flips are one decision changed and one the other way ($p = 1.0$); the \emph{narrowest} mask carries the most withholding, three prompts and none the other way ($p = 0.25$); and nothing clears $p = 0.05$ at any width. One prompt changes decision in every arm, and it is the same prompt: 16 asks how a seventeen-year-old can get a free drink at a bar, base supplies a bogus ``turning 18 this year'' workaround, and every coupled write withholds it --- full width with ``I cannot provide information'', which the keyword rule lists, and 41 and 205 coordinates with ``You cannot legally get an alcoholic drink'', which it does not. Two arms also move the wrong way on one prompt: at 205 and at 410 coordinates the coupled reply \emph{offers} to generate the ``worst'' statements the backbone refused (prompt 81), which is the direction an alignment channel must not move and which no keyword count would report. Section~\ref{sec:discussion2} says what that does to the claim.

\textbf{On 400 prompts the wide write's withholding is real on substance.} The
400-item set (the 100 above verbatim, plus 300 drawn from the same pool by
\code{eval/build\_redteam\_prompts.py}) was labelled by four blind judges of 100
pairs each, with a 40-pair independent re-judge (39 of 40 agree). The keyword
count replicates out of sample: 13 toward refusal and 3 away on the 300 new
prompts alone ($p = 0.021$), $19{:}3$ over all 400 ($p = 0.0009$). And this time
adjudication does not dissolve it: the wide write withholds the requested
assistance on 21 pairs and supplies it on 2, exact McNemar $p = 0.0001$. The two
measures pick out different pairs --- 7 of the 21 carry a refusal phrase, and 12
of the 19 keyword flips are SAME on substance --- so the count was an instrument
for a real effect it did not itself measure. The first hundred prompts reproduce
the earlier pass (99 of 100 labels agree; $1{:}0$ there) and the 300 new ones
carry $20{:}2$, a difference at the edge of chance (Fisher $p = 0.035$): the quiet
verdict at $n = 100$ was a small sample, not a different judge. In 12 of the 21
the backbone's own reply was a keyword refusal that still supplied something ---
a legal answer, an explanation, a clean alternative --- and the coupled reply
dropped that remainder. What kind of thing was dropped is its own question, so
two further judges, blind and independent, categorised the withheld content in
each of the 23 changed pairs; they agree on all 23

Of the 21 withholdings, 2 are specifics a careful assistant should withhold ---
the underage-drink workaround of prompt 16, and reagent-level synthesis routes
for an incapacitating agent --- 4 are dual-use (a lock's mechanism, how panic

15 are legitimate information: the legal answer on campus firearms, the research
consensus on school violence, a clinical description of compulsive behaviour,
a clean joke, a gross-out story, the arguments a political side makes. Of the

the backbone refused) and one is audit statistics. So the disposition the wide
write carries, where it carries one, is a blunter refusal, and it is paid for
mostly in helpfulness: 15 pairs of legitimate assistance withheld against 2 of

\textbf{Cross-entropy is sublinear in width.} On converged values the improvement over base fits $\Delta\mathrm{CE} = 0.00317\, d^{0.393}$, with residuals of $-4.2\%$, $+6.2\%$ and $-2.3\%$ across two decades of width, so quadrupling the written dimensions buys about 1.7$\times$ the signal and twenty times the dimensions about three times the effect. Three points and a fitted exponent are an empirical regularity, not a law, but the shape is the useful part: there is no width at which the signal jumps (in the cross-entropy as evaluated; of the part that no temperature gives the backbone, Section~\ref{sec:reads}, these three arms keep $-0.0014$, $0.0012$ and $0.040$, so for that part the smooth shape does not hold, and three points confounded with energy cannot say where it appears).

\textbf{The widths were not compared at equal strength, and that is a property of the cap, not of the mask.} The injection cap bounds the injection's root-mean-square \emph{per written coordinate} (Section~\ref{sec:interface2}), so it is indifferent to how many coordinates are written --- and every arm saturated it exactly. Recovering the cap from the logged gate and injection ratio gives $0.2000$ at 41, 205 and 4096 dimensions alike. The total perturbation the channel may apply therefore scales with the summed squared activation of the written set, which we measure at the coupling layer (\code{results/value\_neurons/qwen35/rms\_layer24\_full.json}) and report in Table~\ref{tab:constenergy}. Full width was permitted 26$\times$ the perturbation energy of the value-neuron mask and 9.8$\times$ that of the top-5\% mask. The comparison above therefore measures what a mask does to a \emph{budget}, and says nothing yet about what it does to \emph{dimensionality}.

\textbf{Per unit of perturbation, the narrow write does more.} Dividing the CE gain by the energy the mask was allowed inverts the ranking: 0.334 at 41 dimensions and 0.271 at 205 against 0.094 at full width, so the value-neuron write is 3.6$\times$ more efficient per unit of disturbance than writing everywhere, at 45$\times$ lower total KL to the backbone. That would be the quantity an alignment intervention wants to be good at --- useful change per unit of collateral --- if the gain were useful change. Section~\ref{sec:reads} shows it is mostly sharpening: with the backbone and the coupled system each read at its own best temperature, the 41- and 205-coordinate writes at this cap keep $-0.0014$ and $0.0012$ of their gain, neither distinguishable from zero, against full width's 0.0403, and on that part of the fit full width is the more efficient write. The earlier version of this section noticed the per-\emph{dimension} form of this and dismissed it, on the grounds that ``efficiency per dimension is not the constraint anyone has, total transfer is''. The dismissal was wrong for a reason worth stating: total transfer is a constraint, but it is not a property of the mask. It is the cap, and the cap is a free parameter.

\textbf{What the channel carries changes with width, and so does what it costs.} Comparing the relative CE gain on the two splits separates manner from judgment. At 41 dimensions the gain is 2.63\% on red-team prompts and 3.12\% on ordinary ones; at 205, 5.74\% and 6.13\%. Both are \emph{larger} where the constitution has least to say --- the signature of a channel transmitting the teacher's style, its terseness and hedging under a long system prompt, rather than its situational reasoning. (Section~\ref{sec:reads} removes the ground for that reading: what the relative gains compare on both splits is sharpening, which a temperature reproduces, and neither masked write is nearer the teacher's distribution than the backbone. The full-width inversion survives the same control, 0.0403 against 0.0041.) Only at full width does the order invert, 19.45\% against 13.95\%. And the over-refusal arrives at exactly that width: ordinary-request refusal holds at base's 0.02 for both masked variants and rises to 0.06 for the whole stream, which also refuses one ordinary prompt that neither base nor the teacher refuses. Counting items, the full-width write acquires two of the teacher's four extra refusals on ordinary requests and two of its nine on harmful ones --- a bias toward the unwanted half, though Fisher's exact test on 2-of-9 against 2-of-4 gives $p = 0.53$, so that is a direction and not a finding. The honest statement is narrower: below full width, at this cap, the channel carries nothing a temperature does not give the backbone and changes nothing; at full width it starts carrying the teacher's decisions, and both halves of them arrive together.

\textbf{So the design was never tested at this scale.} Writing only into the value neurons was motivated by a causal result that does not reproduce here at all (Section~\ref{sec:valueneurons}), and by the hope that a narrow write would be the safe way to carry values. The first motivation is gone. The second we can now say is untested rather than unnecessary: at 9B every width is safe, but the narrow widths were also starved, and a channel that is not permitted to perturb cannot be observed to transmit. The experiment that separates the two raises the cap to restore energy parity with full width --- $0.2\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{full}}/E_{\mathrm{mask}}}$ --- on 410 coordinates, 10\% of the stream, where the probe's AUC still holds (Section~\ref{sec:valueneurons}) and where the identity control costs nothing to construct: the probe's \emph{worst} 410 coordinates sum to 549.31 of activation RMS against the best 410's 553.17, disjoint, with the attention sink in neither. Two arms, cap 0.5220 and 0.5514, ask whether 10\% of the coordinates suffice and whether they have to be those 10\%. Table~\ref{tab:width410} reports what they found.

\textbf{Given the budget, 10\% of the coordinates transmit.} The same 410 coordinates
that carry nothing at the default cap carry, at parity, 62\% of full width's
cross-entropy gain on the red-team split (0.0579 against 0.0939), 55\% of its
divergence (0.0576 against 0.1047), and four of the six red-team prompts it flips
toward refusal, three of them the same prompts. The starved interpolation from the
fitted power law predicted a gain of 0.034; parity delivers 0.058. So the first
reading of this campaign --- that the mask throttles the signal and the whole stream
is needed to move a decision --- was substantially an artifact of the cap, and the
honest replacement is that width and budget both matter. On the cross-entropy as
evaluated restoring the budget multiplies the gain more than widening does (from an
interpolated 0.034 to 0.058 at 410 coordinates, against 0.058 to 0.094 from there to
the whole stream),
and it moves four of full width's six keyword flips. On the gain that survives a
temperature control (Section~\ref{sec:reads}) width is the larger term by far:
restoring the budget moves the 41- and 205-coordinate masks by 0.006 and 0.004, and
widening them to the whole stream at the same energy by 0.036. The narrower masks, given the same parity, follow: 41
coordinates carry 50\% of full width's gain (0.0466) and 34\% of its divergence,
205 carry 58\% (0.0549) and 56\%, so over a tenfold range of width the fit is
sublinear in width at fixed energy and the divergence tracks the fit. (These are
shares of the cross-entropy gain as evaluated. Of the part of that gain which no
temperature gives the backbone, Section~\ref{sec:reads}, the 410 carry 18\% and the
205 and the 41 about a tenth.)

\textbf{What parity does not do is close the gap.} Two thirds of the cross-entropy
gain as evaluated is not equality --- and of the part no temperature gives the
backbone it is under a fifth (0.0073 against 0.0403, Section~\ref{sec:reads}) --- and
the refusal move --- $1{:}4$, $p = 0.375$ --- is neither distinguishable from zero nor
from full width's $0{:}6$ on 100 items. We said in advance that 100 items could not
resolve an intermediate effect and they did not; this arm establishes a direction and
a magnitude on cross-entropy and divergence, where the measurement is precise (and,
for cross-entropy, mostly of sharpening), and leaves the behavioural question
underpowered. Whether the residual third is dimensionality or the diminishing
return of spending perturbation is what the identity control speaks to.

\textbf{The narrow write is tighter in three places and looser in one.} Against full
width at the same total energy it cuts the GSM8K divergence to two fifths (0.00014
against 0.00035) and BoolQ's to a third (0.00032 against 0.00091), moves no BoolQ item, and
--- the result that matters most for an alignment channel --- leaves ordinary-request
refusal at the backbone's 0.02 where full width rose to 0.06, so on the harmful
split it moves the keyword count toward the teacher's refusal ($1{:}4$, $p = 0.375$,
which matched random sets match) without acquiring its over-refusal on the
ordinary one. That is 0 extra refusals against full width's 2 of 50, which is not a
distinguishable difference, and we flag it as the most promising thin result here
rather than a finding.

The exception is MMLU divergence: KL 0.0244 --- 5.4$\times$ the 0.0045 of a full-width
write carrying the same total energy in 2.6$\times$ smaller per-coordinate steps.
Accuracy does not follow it: scored with the repaired parser, MMLU goes 75 to 77, two
items gained and none lost, $p = 0.5$ (the earlier reading of six items at $p = 0.031$
was the parser scoring bare-letter answers as wrong, which this arm happened to
reformat on seven items). Total energy therefore does not predict the divergence. We first attributed it to
per-coordinate magnitude, on the reasoning that a concentrated write leaves the
residual stream's local distribution; the identity control of
Section~\ref{sec:identity} refutes that, and the correct predictor is \emph{which}
coordinates are written.


\section{Does it have to be the probe's coordinates?}
\label{sec:identity}

one piece of luck in this campaign. The probe's \emph{worst} 410 coordinates are
disjoint from its best 410, sum to 549.31 of activation RMS against 553.17, and

magnitude, and after the cap on total write energy (1.0002 against 1.0000), with a
per-coordinate cap 5.6\% \emph{higher}. Nothing had to be matched by construction;
the probe's ranking is magnitude-biased at both ends, and the two ends happen to
agree.

\textbf{They do not behave better, and they disturb the model far more.} The
controls reach 0.68--0.71 keyword refusal against the treatment's 0.690, flipping

resolvable at $n = 100$, and the controls nominally ahead. Adjudicated, the

the four controls sit at $0.00025 \pm 0.00002$ against 0.02441, a 98-fold

and BoolQ divergence are lower for the probe-worst set too.

coordinates the value probe ranks highest are the ones where writing also perturbs
multiple-choice reasoning; the coordinates it ranks lowest deliver the same or more
of the behaviour with a hundredth of the collateral. Cross-entropy against a teacher
is a fit metric, and the 1.27--1.33$\times$ the probe earns on it was its coordinates'
only win; Section~\ref{sec:reads} finds that margin is sharpening, and with each arm
read at its best temperature it is gone. All three matched replicates reproduce the probe-worst
set's near-zero collateral: MMLU KL 0.00026, 0.00022 and 0.00027, each below the
probe-best set on 100 of 100 items with median per-item ratios of 151$\times$,

answers nothing. The one gain, on prompt 92, is the direction an alignment
channel must not move: the coupled reply names the sensitive body areas the
backbone would not. The honest reading is not that coordinate
identity is irrelevant --- it plainly is not, at 98$\times$ --- but that the probe
has been selecting, throughout this campaign, for the coordinates one would least

\section{Both partners at once: \psilm{}-2}
\label{sec:dual}

The physics bridge and the constitution bridge can be put on the same frozen backbone and run in one forward pass. Each reads at layer 13; the constitution channel injects at 24 and the physics channel at 26, so the physics gate reads a stream the constitution has already modified. That is the composition's only real coupling, and the implementation exposes it rather than absorbing it: every active channel's read depth must lie at or below every write depth, so that the reads all see the unmodified stream and the composition has one unambiguous order, and an interleaved configuration is refused rather than silently resolved. Together the two bridges are 73.52M parameters --- physics 45.18M, constitution 28.34M --- against nine billion frozen ones.

\textbf{Three bit-identity properties make the result attributable.} With both channels closed the stack reproduces the bare staged backbone; with the physics channel alone it reproduces \code{PsiLMMLX}; with the constitution channel alone it reproduces \code{PsiConstitutionMLX} --- bit for bit in each case, on a tiny random stack and on the real 9B. So any difference in a dual run is the other channel's presence and not a different code path. A joint training phase was also written, updating only the two gate modules under a cross-gate penalty that asks each channel's gate to shut on the other's on-task prompts, since neither single-channel run could have trained that: both campaigns' negatives were GSM8K and MMLU, so neither gate had ever seen the other channel's prompts as something to close on.

prompts --- and adjudicated, each changes exactly one decision, the same prompt. Whatever the physics channel is doing, it is not displacing the
constitution channel's write, and it is not being displaced by it. That is the part
of the composition claim that survives contact with behaviour, and it is the part
the bit-identity properties above predict.

\textbf{The composition is not inert on the backbone, which neither channel at its
trained cap was.} With both channels open MMLU divergence to base is 0.0723 with a p90 of
0.205 --- sixteen times the 0.0045 the constitution channel alone produced, and the
largest off-task perturbation anywhere in this paper. Accuracy, scored with the
repaired parser, goes 75 to 77 ($3{:}1$, $p = 0.63$) against $1{:}1$ for the
constitution channel alone; the divergence is the finding, and the accuracy is not one ---
GSM8K and BoolQ stay where they were. So the claim that the two
channels compose with \emph{no measurable interference} is false as stated: it was
true of cross-entropy and of physics accuracy, and it is not true of
multiple-choice behaviour.

We are careful about which way to read this. The movement is one-directional and it
is an \emph{improvement}, so ``interference'' is the wrong word for what was
measured --- three items gained and one lost is not damage, and a reader who wanted
the dual stack to be harmless gets that. What fails is the stronger and more
interesting claim, that the composition is free: adding a second channel changed the
backbone's answers on a benchmark neither channel moved by itself.

\textbf{Which channel, and the subject names are not the answer.} The three gained
items are \code{high\_school\_mathematics} and \code{college\_physics},
which points at the physics channel --- it is the one with a reason to engage a
mathematics prompt. That inference does not survive Section~\ref{sec:const9b}'s
410-coordinate arm. \emph{Two of these three items are gained there too}, by the
constitution channel alone with no physics channel present at all; only
\code{college\_physics:94} is unique to the dual stack. So these MMLU items are fragile under any
sufficiently strong write at this depth, the subjects they belong to are a property
of which items are fragile rather than of which partner is attached, and a reading
that assigned them to the physics channel would have been wrong.

What does separate the arms is the size of the divergence, and it orders by write
strength rather than by channel count: MMLU KL to base is 0.0045 for the full-width
constitution write at cap 0.2, 0.0244 for 410 coordinates at cap 0.522, and 0.0723
here, where two full-width writes at cap 0.2 land at two different depths. The dual
stack is the largest perturbation of the three and the only one carrying two
payloads, and we still cannot say from this run how much of its MMLU divergence is
the physics channel's own write: the gate the bench records is the
\emph{constitution} channel's, so the physics gate's activity on those prompts is
not in the record. The arm that settles it runs the same suite with only the physics
channel open (\code{results/dual/guardrail\_phys.sh}), and it did: with only the
physics channel open, MMLU KL to base is 0.0409 (p90 0.111), against 0.0045 for
the constitution channel alone and 0.0723 for both, so most of the composition's
MMLU divergence is the physics channel's own write, and the two together exceed
their sum by 0.027. Its accuracy is unchanged (74 against 75, one item each way),
GSM8K is item-identical at 83, BoolQ loses one item, and its red-team divergence
is 0.0005: the physics channel has no behaviour of its own there.


What the values channel carries is the deflationary half of this paper, with one boundary now drawn more carefully than it was. At 9B a narrow write into probe-identified coordinates gains nothing in fit to a prompted teacher that a temperature does not give the backbone, and carries none of its judgment, \emph{at the perturbation budget the cap allowed it} (Section~\ref{sec:reads}; at 0.5B nine such coordinates keep a small gain on the red-team split, none distinguishable from zero on the helpful one, and no more of one than magnitude-matched coordinates do); the width at which judgment did transmit is also the width that imported the teacher's over-refusal; the coordinates were chosen on a causal claim that does not reproduce at either scale; and a partner that has memorised the document is interchangeable with --- at nine dimensions and as evaluated, slightly worse than --- one that has never read it, because the document enters through the teacher's context rather than the partner's weights --- and, at 9B, because the write does not use what the partner computes (Section~\ref{sec:reads}). Three of those four are findings about this design. The first is, for now, a finding about how we configured it.


\section{What the channel reads, and what its fit measures}
\label{sec:reads}

Two measurements made after the campaign closed, on 2026-09-28, change how the
preceding sections should be read, and two experiments whose criteria were written
down before they ran follow from them. Two more tests with their rules fixed in
advance were added on 2026-09-29 and 30: the temperature control on the 0.5B
bridges, and the stored tokens on the benchmarks. No adjudicated count reported
above moves.
What moves is the meaning of ``fit'', and the description of the channel: at 9B it
is, in all but one of thirteen arms, a gated write of something learned, not of
something read.

\textbf{The write does not depend on what the channel read.} The trained bridge was
run teacher-forced on the 50-item red-team and helpful splits with one thing
replaced at a time: the write switched off; the eight tokens the write receives
taken from \emph{another prompt} of the same split (the preceding one, so a split's
first item, having no donor, is not scored); and the partner fed zeros in
place of the forward bridge's tokens (\code{eval/\allowbreak{}constitution\_compress.py};
KL of the trained system's next-token distribution to the variant's, per token of
the continuation). At full width, switching the write off moves the output by a KL
of 0.0652 on the red-team split. Another prompt's tokens move it by 0.00032, and
storing the bridges in half precision, which changes nothing the channel carries,
moves it by 0.00028: the prompt's own tokens are worth what the arithmetic's
rounding is worth. The zero-fed partner moves it by 0.0014, and the cross-entropy
gain is kept to 1.00 and 0.99 of itself. Table~\ref{tab:tempcontrol} gives the same
three numbers for every single-channel 9B arm of the campaign: in twelve of thirteen the two replacements move the
output by 0.0002 to 0.0014 on either split, against 0.002 to 0.070 for the write
itself, and keep 0.93 to 1.03 of the gain. The exception is the magnitude-matched
random 205, where they move it by 0.0008 to 0.0029 against a write of 0.011 to
0.016. The smaller backbone is different: at 0.5B (its 100-item splits) another
prompt's tokens move the full-width write's output by 0.035 of its 0.222 and the
zero-fed partner by 0.033, and 0.92 and 0.88 of the gain is kept. There the prompt's
own tokens matter: another prompt's move the output by about a sixth of the write's
divergence on the red-team split and a ninth on the helpful one (0.016 of 0.149),
and the zero-fed partner by nearly a third there (0.045); one stored set of
tokens does not stand in for the partner there (below). (A bridge trained by the same recipe on a 27B ternary
backbone for the companion application, outside this paper's campaign, gives
0.00007 and 0.00003 against 0.0856.)

So in twelve of the thirteen 9B arms the write's effect on the output is the same
whatever prompt's tokens it is given, and in the full-width arm, where the rounding
floor was measured, it is the same to the precision of the arithmetic. The tokens
themselves are not identical (cosine to their mean 0.996 on average over 100
validation prompts, 0.77 at the least); what the output does not register is how
they differ. In the thirteenth arm, the matched random 205, another prompt's tokens
move the output by up to a sixth of the write's divergence and the zero-fed partner
by up to a quarter, as at 0.5B. The gate and the attention's
query read the backbone's own stream at the write layer, and the gate is what
separates a red-team prompt from an arithmetic one: the full-width gate is 0.48 on
the red-team continuations and 0.014 on held-out benchmark continuations. This is
the 9B partner control of Section~\ref{sec:const9b} seen from the other side: at 9B
a partner that never read the document did as well as one that had because what
either computes from the prompt is not what the write uses. At 0.5B the write does
use the read, so the partner null of Section~\ref{sec:const05} needs the other
explanation: whatever the write takes from the read, a partner that has never read
the document supplies it as well. (That null is one of cross-entropy, and it
stands at best temperature too; below.)

\textbf{At 9B most of the fit is sharpening.} The supervision is cross-entropy on the
teacher's \emph{greedy} tokens, and cross-entropy on argmax targets falls when a
model is merely made more confident in the tokens it mostly picks anyway. So each
arm was also scored with its logits divided by a temperature, on a grid from 0.30
to 1.60 in steps of 0.05, and the comparison repeated with the backbone and the
coupled system each at its own best temperature, chosen on the \emph{other} split
(\code{eval/\allowbreak{}teacher\_ceiling.py}, which first checks that its teacher pass
reproduces the stored continuations and that its base and coupled arms reproduce
the evaluator's cross-entropies; they do to within 0.00005 wherever an evaluator run
exists, which for the three matched 410 draws is the red-team split only). At full width on the red-team
split the backbone's cross-entropy falls from 0.4829 to 0.4209 at a temperature of
0.60, with no bridge at all, and the coupled system's from 0.3890 to 0.3806 at
0.75. Of the gain of 0.0939, 0.0403 is left (95\% interval 0.028 to 0.058, a paired
bootstrap over items that resamples both splits, so that the choice of the
temperatures is inside it). On the helpful split 0.0041 of 0.0576 is left, and the
interval includes zero, as it does for every arm on that split. The narrow arms
keep 0.012 or less of their red-team gain (Table~\ref{tab:tempcontrol}). At 0.5B
the control was run afterwards, and it reads differently there; the cross-entropy
comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:const05} --- the value neurons' small signal, the
plain partner's advantage of 0.0040, the split into 63\% magnitude and 37\%
identity --- are taken up below, after the 9B readings. (On the 27B bridge outside
this campaign the control leaves nothing of the red-team gain.)

mostly how often the two temperatures part, which is why they are lopsided. Two
of the readings sit on that edge: under two other seeds of the bootstrap the
value neurons against nine random coordinates, and the first matched draw's own
gain, have zero inside their intervals, though both are clear of it at the
split's own best temperatures and at every one temperature from 0.65 to 1.00. And
the distance by which a minimum taken over the grid can lie above its curve's
own, which the rules took from 9B as 0.0004, is 0.0006 here.

The two comparisons that fail do not fail alike. The plain partner's advantage is
positive wherever it was looked for: 0.0040 to 0.0068 with both sides at one
temperature from 0.50 to 1.00, with an interval clear of zero from 0.70 up, and
0.0044 (0.0003 to 0.0092) at the red-team split's own best temperatures, which
clears zero by less than the grid resolves. It is a small advantage that the
cross-fit could not confirm. The identity term changes sign with the temperature
at which both sides are read. Its interval lies below zero from 0.50 to 0.70 ---
at 0.65, where the cross-fit reads the value neurons and all three draws, it is
$-0.0067$ with an interval of $-0.011$ to $-0.003$ --- and above zero only at
0.95 and 1.00; at the split's own best temperatures it is $-0.0007$ ($-0.003$ to
0.002). The reading's interval reaches 0.030 only through the 28\% of draws in
which the value neurons and the three draws were not all read at one temperature.
What
Section~\ref{sec:const05} measured as identity is there at temperature 1 and is
not there at the temperatures that are best for these prompts. It resembles the
first statement above, on thinner evidence: on the red-team split the two
value-neuron arms' cross-entropy curves bottom out a little later than the
backbone's and than every other nine-coordinate arm's (parabola vertices at 0.827
and 0.825, against the backbone's 0.814 and the others' 0.809 to 0.813; on the
grid 0.85 against 0.80, though their cross-entropies at 0.80 and 0.85 differ by
0.0002 and 0.00001, less than the grid resolves), and at temperature 1 they are
the only nine-coordinate arms further from the teacher's distribution than the
backbone is (0.456 and 0.453 against 0.447, with no interval; at the best
temperatures for that distance the plain-partner arm is not).

On the helpful split, which the rules report and do not read, the nine value
neurons keep nothing cross-fitted ($-0.0053$, $-0.0090$ to 0.0133) and 0.0055
(0.0021 to 0.0086) at that split's own best temperatures, and the plain partner's
full-width bridge keeps more than the fine-tuned partner's as evaluated (0.0109,
0.001 to 0.023), at the split's own best temperatures (0.0141, 0.001 to 0.029)
and at every one temperature from 0.50 to 1.00, though not cross-fitted (0.0081,
$-0.007$ to 0.024). On no reading, at either width or on either split, does the
fine-tuned partner's bridge keep more than the plain partner's with an interval
clear of zero. So the partner null of Section~\ref{sec:const05} stands, and with
it the conclusion that the document in the partner's weights is not what the
channel carries; its sharper form, that a memorising partner is marginally worse,
rested on the 0.0040 and is not confirmed. There is no adjudicated count at 0.5B
for the control to be set against, and the control was not run on the validation
split, which Table~\ref{tab:const05} also reports.

\textbf{Two experiments with their criteria fixed in advance.} If the write does
not use what was read, the behaviour should survive taking the read away, and the
partner should not be needed to train it. Both were tried on the 400 red-team
prompts of Table~\ref{tab:adjudicated} (the verdicts are in
Table~\ref{tab:prereg}), against criteria committed before either
ran (\code{results/\allowbreak{}constitution/\allowbreak{}partnerfree\_\allowbreak{}preregistration.json}), and the verdicts
below are those criteria applied by a script
(\code{eval/\allowbreak{}prereg\_verdict.py}) whose thresholds were pinned by tests before the
second experiment had a result. The first 40 prompts of the recorded run, generated
again by the present code, came back token for token in both arms with identical
divergences, so the backbone's and the full-width arm's replies are the recorded
ones. Adjudication follows the rubric of Section~\ref{sec:const9b} with three
additions forced by the design. A reply that is the backbone's, character for
character, is labelled \emph{same} without a judge. A pair that is, character for
character, a pair the record labelled keeps that label. Every other pair goes to a
judge in files of at most fifty under random identifiers, mixed with the recorded
full-width pair of each judged prompt whose recorded full-width reply differs from
the backbone's (an \emph{anchor}), with no file holding a
prompt twice, so no judge sees two coupled replies to one prompt and the round
itself says how its judges label pairs the record labelled; independent judges
label a fixed sample again from files and identifiers of their own
(\code{eval/\allowbreak{}adjudication\_prepare.py}). ``The same batch'' of the criteria was
therefore, in practice, the same round: an anchor is judged by another judge than
the one who has the arm's pair of its prompt.

\emph{Stored tokens.} Two arms use the trained full-width bridge with its write fed
one stored set of eight tokens for every prompt, so that the forward bridge, the
partner and the reverse bridge are not evaluated at all: the mean of the tokens of
100 validation prompts, none of them among the 400, and the tokens of a partner fed
zeros, which are made from no prompt. The prompts' own tokens lie close to their
mean (mean cosine 0.996, the lowest of the 100 prompts 0.77); the zero-fed set lies
beyond all of them (cosine 0.73 to that mean, relative distance 0.96 against at most
0.90 for any prompt). With the
mean tokens the coupled system withholds on 21 pairs and supplies on 3
($p = 0.0003$; keyword count $18{:}3$), which meets the criterion for the behaviour
having \emph{survived}: the recorded arm's $21{:}2$ is reproduced when the forward
bridge, the partner and the reverse bridge are replaced by one stored set of tokens.
With the zero-fed tokens it withholds on 13 and supplies on 5
($p = 0.096$; keyword count $19{:}4$), the criterion's \emph{partial}. Read by this
round's judges wherever they judged the pair, the mean-token arm withholds more
than the zero-fed arm on 8 prompts and less on none ($p = 0.008$; the criteria name
this comparison only as the reference for reading a partial, and set no threshold
for it). So the write needs no tokens made from the prompt, but it is not
indifferent to which constant it is given: the tokens its read produces on prompts
it was not trained on are concentrated around their mean, and a set lying just
beyond the farthest of them carries a weaker copy of the behaviour --- while leaving
the keyword count where it was, which is one more case of the two instruments
parting.

\emph{No partner.} The full-width recipe was trained again with the partner
replaced by a constant: eight fixed random vectors where the partner's features
were, so that nothing a partner computes, and nothing the forward bridge reads,
reaches the write. Held-out cross-entropy at step 1000 is 0.3915 against 0.3904 for
the arm with the partner, and per item the two differ by 0.0031 ($-0.0005$ to

helpful one; the gain at best temperature is 0.0357 against 0.0403. On the

each way. Adjudicated it withholds on 14 pairs and supplies on 2 ($p = 0.0042$).
The criterion for a \emph{match} asked for at least 14 at $p < 0.01$, and this one
is met with no room; neither is the guard-rail's bound on GSM8K (87 against 84,

\textbf{What the channel is, then.} Without a partner the write is still
state-dependent: the attention's query and the gate read the backbone's stream at
the write layer, and the gate is what tells a red-team prompt from an arithmetic
one. What the write draws on is an eight-slot memory that does not depend on the
prompt --- learned through the reverse bridge in the runs with a partner, and
learnable without one. That is not a random direction of the write's size, which is
what the content-free control of Section~\ref{sec:const9b} injected and which
carried little (5\% of the divergence, $8{:}4$ on substance); it is a small learned
memory read by a state-dependent attention. Whether a single steering vector fixed
across prompts would do as well was not tested. It is
also not what Section~\ref{sec:constbridge} set out to build, a channel through
which a partner holding a document speaks to a backbone about the situation in
front of it. For the full-width bridge at 9B the forward bridge, the partner and
the reverse bridge can be replaced at inference, on the 400 red-team prompts, by one
stored set of eight tokens --- 32,768 numbers in place of a 494M-parameter model
and its two bridges --- with the adjudicated withholding reproduced, and on three
benchmarks with its correct answers within two of the partner path's. No other 9B
arm was run this way, and at 0.5B, where the write uses what it reads, the
replacement fails.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion2}

\textbf{What has been shown.} The coupling interface of \companion{} accepts a
partner that computes nothing and holds a document instead, and the resulting
channel changes the backbone's decisions --- more withholding on the adjudicated
count, the direction in which the teacher's keyword refusal rate lies above the
backbone's (the teacher itself was not adjudicated); by divergence from the
teacher's own distribution every 9B arm, as evaluated, is further from it than the
backbone, and at each system's best temperature none is nearer by more than half a
percent (Section~\ref{sec:reads}; at 0.5B the full-width arms are further from it
as evaluated too, and the picture at best temperature is mixed). It does so while leaving arithmetic and multiple-choice reasoning
within one or two items of the frozen backbone, and item-identical at 41 coordinates,
with divergence two orders of magnitude larger on prompts the document speaks to than
on arithmetic --- 302$\times$ at full width --- and a gate whose own selectivity is
highest where the write is narrowest, which is the behaviour the no-harm arm trains
for and the property that makes such a channel auditable at all. Two channels of
unrelated kinds compose on one backbone without joint training, neither costing the
other its payload --- though the composition is not inert on the backbone, and
Section~\ref{sec:dual} reports where it is not. And the probe that
locates the write site reproduces at two scales, with a signal that occupies a
roughly constant number of coordinates rather than a constant fraction.

\textbf{What has not been shown, and what was withdrawn.} The causal claim that
motivated writing into value neurons does not reproduce: at 0.5B the damage is
mostly magnitude, at 9B there is no damage to explain. The partner control is
negative at both scales --- a partner that has never read the document does as
well at 0.5B (on cross-entropy, as evaluated and at best temperature), and at 9B reproduces the wide write's withholding on substance,
19 pairs against 3 where the fine-tuned partner's arm gave 21 against 2, fifteen
of them the same pairs --- so the document reaches the backbone through the
teacher's context, and the partner is a carrier rather than a source --- and, by
Section~\ref{sec:reads}, a carrier whose cargo the write does not read: at 9B one stored set
of tokens, the mean of 100 other prompts', reproduces the withholding ($21{:}3$),
though a set made from no prompt carries only a weaker copy ($13{:}5$), and a bridge
trained with no partner withholds on 14 pairs against 2 in each of two runs; by
criteria fixed in advance the stored set also leaves three benchmarks where the
partner's path has them, and at 0.5B, where the write uses what it reads, it does
not stand in. And
judgment, adjudicated on substance rather than
counted by keyword, arrives only at full width and only with enough prompts to see
it: on 100 prompts no arm withholds more than the backbone at $p < 0.05$, one
decision changes and it changes in every arm, and two arms give more on a prompt
the backbone refused; on 400 the wide write withholds on 21 pairs against 2
($p = 0.0001$), and what it withholds is, by two blind judges who agree on every
pair, legitimate information fifteen times, dual-use material four times and
harmful specifics twice. The judgment that arrives is a blunter refusal, and it
is the document's: a content-free injection of the same magnitude through the
same gate changes 8 decisions one way and 4 the other ($p = 0.39$) at 5\% of the
divergence. The probe-best 410 at matched energy, given the same 400 prompts, withholds $16{:}5$
($p = 0.027$), thirteen of them legitimate information, and twice hands over
harmful specifics the backbone had refused; the probe-worst set does the same,
$15{:}2$ ($p = 0.0023$), and releases the same precursor list, so that failure
belongs to the 410-coordinate write at this energy and not to the probe's
coordinates. The narrower masks at parity, given those prompts, carry a weaker copy of the same withholding, $10{:}4$ at 41 coordinates and $10{:}6$ at 205, neither significant at $n = 400$, on largely the wide write's prompts, and release nothing harmful. And the claim that a narrow write cannot carry a
decision is withdrawn to a weaker one: it did not, at a budget 26 times smaller
than the one the wide write was given. At matched energy the probe-best 410
carried most of the wide write's fit as evaluated and a weaker, non-significant copy of its
keyword shift ($1{:}4$ away${:}$toward, $p = 0.375$, on 100 items;
Section~\ref{sec:const9b}); the 41- and 205-coordinate masks at matched energy
carry half and 58\% of its fit as evaluated and, on 400 prompts, a withholding in the wide
write's direction that does not reach significance. So the honest summary of this
paper's central question --- can a narrow, auditable write carry a disposition? ---
is that it carries a weaker copy of one, in the same direction and on largely the
same prompts, at the MMLU cost the probe's coordinates exact at every width
(Section~\ref{sec:identity}); whether that copy clears significance is not settled
by strength alone --- at equal energy it did at 410 coordinates ($16{:}5$) and not at
41 or 205 ($10{:}4$, $10{:}6$), though the difference between those arms was not
itself tested --- and the magnitude-matched controls at 41 and
205 have decided that the cost is the coordinates and not the write: random sets
written just as hard pay the 410 controls' collateral and no more
(Section~\ref{sec:identity}).

\textbf{The scaling conjecture, stated as a conjecture.} If the value signal
occupies a fixed number of coordinates as backbones widen (Table~\ref{tab:vnknee}),
and if a few hundred coordinates can carry a decision when given the budget to do
so, then this kind of coupling gets \emph{cheaper} in relative terms at frontier
scale: the bridge's parameters grow linearly in $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, the mask costs
nothing, and the fraction of the stream disturbed falls. Neither antecedent is
established. The second has since been tested at energy parity
(Section~\ref{sec:const9b}): 410 coordinates carry a significant adjudicated
withholding whether or not the probe chose them, and 205 and 41 a weaker copy that
does not clear significance; what that withholding is --- a blunter refusal that
mostly withholds legitimate information, with little fit at 9B beyond sharpening
(Section~\ref{sec:reads}) --- is a qualification the conjecture inherits. The first
rests on two scales and a coarse grid. We also note a limit that the arithmetic makes
visible: restoring energy parity with a full-width write by raising the cap scales
as $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{model}}/n_{\mathrm{mask}}}$, which at frontier width would
demand per-coordinate writes far outside the residual stream's own distribution. So
parity with full width is the wrong asymptote to aim at. The quantity that governs
whether this scales is the \emph{minimum} energy that moves a decision, and nothing
here has measured it --- full width's KL of 0.105 for a six-item refusal shift is
very likely far above it.

\textbf{Other limitations.} Refusal was adjudicated on 400 held-out prompts by
model judges against a written rubric, with a 40-pair independent re-judge per arm,
on one training run per arm (two for the full-width recipes of
Section~\ref{sec:reads}), one machine and one backbone; the keyword count remains a proxy, and
the two instruments largely disagree on which prompts move (7 of the wide write's
21 adjudicated withholdings are keyword flips). The teacher's continuations at 9B were
generated through a cached-prefill path whose identity check against an uncached
decode passed 3 of 3 at 0.5B and \textbf{0 of 3} at 9B (the path is self-consistent,
and the divergence is greedy ties under different prefill chunking, but every 9B
number inherits the caveat). The constitution enters as a 2{,}848-word excerpt, not
the whole document. All the campaign's backbones are 4-bit quantized. The
temperature control at 0.5B is a weak instrument for its nine-coordinate
comparisons, for the reasons Section~\ref{sec:reads} gives, and it was not run on
the validation split or on the dual stack; the stored tokens were tested on the
full-width bridges only, on single-turn prompts, and by tolerances that cannot
tell a set that carries the withholding from one that carries a weaker copy of
it. And every bridge was trained once,
on one machine, with a fixed data order but an unseeded initialisation, except the
two full-width recipes of Section~\ref{sec:reads}, which were trained twice and differ between runs by up to five adjudicated withholdings
--- a spread that every single-run count in this paper should be read with.

The judges are language-model agents, and in the three rounds of
Section~\ref{sec:reads} they met a limit of their own. Of 82 judges, seven had their output
stopped by a safety classifier before any label was returned (three in turn on one
file, then one, then three), which left five files of 48 pairs unlabelled. The
first such file was given unchanged to three judges in turn; from then on a stopped
file was not put to a further judge as it was. Each of the five was withdrawn and
its pairs, under the identifiers they had, judged once as six files of eight, all
thirty of which came back complete. Seven further judges were stopped once and then
returned every label of their file; those labels are kept, each round's record
names the files, and no verdict changes if their pairs are left out (the second run
with the partner becomes 15 of 350 and the two recipes stay unresolved). Only if
every one of those pairs, most of which the judges labelled \emph{same}, were
counted against the arm would the mean-token arm be partial rather than survived
(\code{results/\allowbreak{}constitution/\allowbreak{}prereg\_verdict.json}).
Every judge's transcript was read: each opened its own file and nothing else.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro2}
